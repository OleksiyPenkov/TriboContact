Classical Hertz Contact Baselines

Analytical homogeneous baselines and physical bounds for coated indentation

For every simulation case, TriboContact computes two closed-form Hertz reference solutions alongside the layered solvers: hertz_substrate (bare substrate properties Es, νs) and hertz_top_film (semi-infinite top-film properties E1, ν1). When coating thickness is much smaller than the contact radius (h / a → 0), the coated response approaches hertz_substrate; when the coating is thick compared with the contact radius (h / a » 1), it approaches hertz_top_film.

Governing Analytical Equations

For a sphere of radius R (material Eb, νb) in frictionless elastic contact with a homogeneous half-space (material Et, νt), the combined reduced modulus E* is:

1E* = 1 − νt2Et + 1 − νb2Eb   (rigid ball: second term = 0)
(1)

Under normal load FN, contact radius a, indenter approach δ, peak pressure p0, mean pressure pmean, and radial pressure profile p(r) follow:

a = ( 3 FN R4 E* )1/3,   δ = a2R,   p0 = 3 FN2 π a2 = 32 pmean
(2)
p(r) = p0 √(1 − (r/a)2)   for r ≤ a,    p(r) = 0  for r > a
(3)

Subsurface Centerline Stresses & Yield Onset

Along the symmetry axis (r = 0, normalized depth ζ = z / a), the principal compressive stresses and von Mises equivalent stress are:

σzz = −p01 + ζ2,   σrr = σθθ = −p0 [ (1 + ν)(1 − ζ arctan(1/ζ)) − 12(1 + ζ2) ]
(4)
σvM(0, z) = |σzz − σrr| = 2 τmax(0, z)
(5)

For Poisson's ratio ν = 0.30, σvM reaches its subsurface peak of approximately 0.620 p0 at depth z ≈ 0.481 a. Therefore, initial yielding (σvM = σY) occurs at:

p0,yield ≈ σY0.620 ≈ 1.61 σY   (⇔  pmean,yield ≈ 1.07 σY)
(6)
Assumptions & Limitations
Classical Hertz theory assumes frictionless contact, infinite half-space geometry, small slopes (a « R), homogeneous isotropic linear elasticity, and no coatings. In coated specimens, modulus mismatch redistributes contact pressure and concentrates radial bending/tensile stresses across interfaces; when σvM exceeds σY, Engine 2 FEM is needed to model plastic spreading.
