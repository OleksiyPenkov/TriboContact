Verification, Validation & Literature References

Automated V&V benchmark acceptance tolerances (T1–T8) and foundational scientific literature

Verification & Validation Matrix (TriboContactCLI validate)

Every release of TriboContact must pass all eight quantitative benchmarks in the automated test suite:

ID Benchmark Acceptance Criterion
T1 Engine 1 homogeneous Hertz limit (Ef = Es, νf = νs, 1, 2, 3 layers) a, δ, p0 within < 0.2% of Hertz; centerline σvM within < 0.5%
T2 Engine 1 thin & thick asymptotes (h / a = 0.001 and h / a = 20) Within < 1% of hertz_substrate and hertz_top_film respectively
T3 O'Sullivan & King (1988) layered elastic trends (Ef / Es ∈ {0.5, 2, 4}, h / a ∈ {0.5, 1.0, 2.0}) Monotonic stiffening/softening trends, interface jump, and match vs. elastic Engine 2 within < 3%
T4 Hamilton (1983) 3D sliding sphere (μ = 0.25, 0.5, homogeneous) Trailing-edge surface peak tensile stress σxx(−a, 0, 0) within < 1% of analytical formula
T5 Engine 2 CalculiX homogeneous elastic Hertz a, δ, p0 within < 2% of Hertz; pressure-integral force balance error < 2%
T6 Cross-solver agreement (Engine 1 vs. elastic Engine 2, Ef / Es = 4 and 10) a, δ, pmax, and interface σrr(z1−), τrz(z1) within < 3%
T7 Engine 2 elastic-plastic validation Homogeneous yield onset near p0 ≈ 1.6 σY; Tabor plateau pmean ≈ 2.8–3.0 σY; PEEQ > 0; unloading hr > 0; coated stack shows lower pmax, wider a, and higher film bending σrr than elastic
T8 FEM mesh convergence (coarse vs. standard vs. fine) Change in pmax and a between standard and fine is < 2%

Scientific References

  1. Hertz, H. (1882). “Über die Berührung fester elastischer Körper.” Journal für die reine und angewandte Mathematik (Crelle), 92, 156–171. (Classical elastic sphere-on-half-space contact theory.)
  2. Burmister, D. M. (1945). “The general theory of stresses and displacements in layered systems I–III.” Journal of Applied Physics, 16, 89–94, 126–127, 296–302. (Axisymmetric Hankel-transform boundary-value solution for multilayer elastic media.)
  3. Tabor, D. (1951). The Hardness of Metals. Oxford University Press. (Empirical constraint factor H ≈ 2.8–3.0 σY and fully plastic indentation plateau.)
  4. Chen, W. T. (1971). “Computation of stresses and displacements in a layered elastic medium.” International Journal of Engineering Science, 9(9), 775–800. (Numerical Hankel quadrature and multilayer elasticity.)
  5. Hamilton, G. M. (1983). “Explicit equations for the stresses beneath a sliding spherical contact.” Proceedings of the Institution of Mechanical Engineers, Part C: Journal of Mechanical Engineering Science, 197(1), 53–59. (Closed-form sliding stress fields and trailing-edge tensile peak validation target for T4.)
  6. Johnson, K. L. (1985). Contact Mechanics. Cambridge University Press. (Comprehensive reference for Hertzian fields, yield onset at p0 ≈ 1.6 σY, and tangential traction contact mechanics.)
  7. O'Sullivan, T. C., & King, R. B. (1988). “Sliding contact stress field due to a spherical indenter on a layered elastic half-space.” ASME Journal of Tribology, 110(2), 235–240. (Benchmark trends for layered stiffness ratios Ef / Es, coating thicknesses h / a, and sliding interface stress jumps.)
  8. Nogi, T., & Kato, T. (1997). “Influence of a hard surface layer on the limit of elastic contact — Part I: Analysis using a real surface model.” ASME Journal of Tribology, 119(3), 493–500. (Frequency-domain multilayer Green's functions and interface stress evaluation.)
  9. Polonsky, I. A., & Keer, L. M. (1999). “A numerical method for solving rough contact problems based on the multi-level multi-summation and conjugate gradient techniques.” Wear, 231(2), 206–219. (Active-set Conjugate Gradient complementarity solver used in Engine 1.)
  10. Dhondt, G. (2004). The Finite Element Method for Three-Dimensional Thermomechanical Applications. Wiley / CalculiX CrunchiX Documentation (ccx v2.23). (Axisymmetric 2° wedge convention, SURFACE TO SURFACE contact, NLGEOM, and J2 *PLASTIC formulation used in Engine 2.)
